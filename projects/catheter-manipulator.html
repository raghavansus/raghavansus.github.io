PROJECT CASE STUDY

Catheter Manipulation Hardware

Mechanical and electromechanical development for controlled catheter translation, rotation, and force feedback.

ADD HERO IMAGE / VIDEO

Context & objective

The master–slave endovascular testbed needed a manipulation mechanism that could reliably translate and rotate a catheter or guidewire on the slave side, and — looking ahead — reflect the forces of that interaction back to the operator. The objective was to design that hardware to SOLIDWORKS-modeled tolerances and validate it against real interaction forces, so the mechanism would hold up across repeated human-factors testing.

My contribution

  • Designed and prototyped a haptic force-feedback system in SOLIDWORKS, characterizing endovascular interaction forces across 100+ trials.
  • Developed the mechanical design for controlled catheter translation and rotation, integrated with the platform's ESP32-based motor control.
  • Applied DFM (Design for Manufacturing) principles throughout to keep the mechanism manufacturable and serviceable for repeated study use.

Design & process

I modeled the manipulation hardware in SOLIDWORKS, iterating on the translation/rotation mechanism and the force-sensing path that feeds the haptic feedback system. Characterizing interaction forces across 100+ trials gave me a working picture of the force ranges the mechanism needed to sense and reproduce, which shaped both the structural design and the sensor placement.

Results & impact

The resulting hardware is the physical manipulation layer of the Remote Endovascular Surgery Testbed, and the force-characterization data now informs how force feedback will be layered into future iterations of the platform.

Reflection

Characterizing real interaction forces before finalizing the mechanical design paid off — it kept the haptic system grounded in measured data instead of assumptions. The next step is closing the loop end-to-end: feeding live force-sensor data back through the master device during actual navigation tasks.
