PROJECT CASE STUDY

Motion Scaling Study

A 16-participant human-factors study investigating how motion scaling affects endovascular navigation.

ADD HERO IMAGE / RESULTS

Context & objective

Motion scaling — the ratio between how far an operator moves an input device and how far the instrument actually travels — is a key design parameter for teleoperated endovascular platforms, but its effect on operator performance isn't well quantified. The goal of this study was to measure how different scaling levels affect accuracy, task performance, and workload during simulated catheter/guidewire navigation.

My contribution

  • Designed and conducted a 16-participant human-factors study across 4 scaling conditions and 5 navigation targets.
  • Built the study protocol and metrics to quantify accuracy, task performance, and workload for each condition.
  • Translated the resulting data into a research manuscript currently in progress.

Design & process

The study runs on the Remote Endovascular Surgery Testbed, using its 4 selectable motion-scaling levels, with the SOFA-based guidewire simulation standing in for a physical output device. Each of the 16 participants navigated to 5 targets under all 4 scaling conditions, giving a within-subject comparison of accuracy, completion time, and perceived workload across scaling levels.

Results & impact

The study produced a controlled, within-subject dataset on how motion scaling shapes operator performance in endovascular navigation — findings that are now being written up as a research manuscript and that directly inform interface design choices for the broader testbed platform.

Reflection

Running a 16-participant study end-to-end — from protocol design through data collection to manuscript writing — sharpened how I think about experimental controls and what counts as a meaningful effect versus noise. The next step is extending the study to include force feedback as an additional variable.
