PROJECT CASE STUDY

Remote Endovascular Surgery Testbed

A modular master–slave robotic platform for studying operator interfaces and motion scaling in endovascular navigation.

ADD HERO IMAGE / VIDEO

Context & objective

Master–slave robotic platforms let a surgeon operate a catheter or guidewire remotely, but interface choices — like how much an operator's hand motion is scaled down before it reaches the instrument — directly affect precision, task performance, and workload. At CERLAB (Carnegie Mellon University), the goal was to build a testbed capable of switching between multiple motion-scaling conditions on demand, so those effects could be measured directly in a controlled human-factors study rather than inferred from prior literature.

My contribution

As a graduate research student, I led the hardware and controls development of the platform from concept through a working, study-ready system.

  • Designed and built an ESP32-based master–slave endovascular robotic platform with 4 selectable motion-scaling levels for catheter and guidewire manipulation.
  • Implemented closed-loop motor control achieving <2° steady-state error, and developed validation methods that reduced debugging time by ~50%.
  • Integrated >200 Hz sensor feedback to keep the master and slave sides synchronized in real time.

Design & process

The platform pairs an operator-facing master device with a motorized slave mechanism, connected over an ESP32-based control loop. I iterated on the motor control architecture to hit sub-2° steady-state accuracy, and built out validation tooling — logging, sensor checks, and repeatable test routines — that made it dramatically faster to isolate hardware and firmware issues during development. High-rate (>200 Hz) sensor feedback keeps master and slave motion tightly coupled across all four scaling conditions.

Results & impact

The completed testbed became the hardware backbone for two related efforts: a 16-participant human-factors study quantifying how motion scaling affects accuracy, task performance, and workload (see Motion Scaling Study), and a SOFA-based simulated vessel environment built as an alternative output device for the same platform (see Guidewire Navigation Simulation).

Reflection

This project pushed me deep into real-time embedded control and sensor synchronization under tight timing constraints. The natural next step is integrating the haptic force-feedback subsystem I prototyped separately (see Catheter Manipulation Hardware) directly into the master-side controls, closing the loop between what the simulated or physical vessel "feels" and what the operator feels at the input device.
